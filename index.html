Monitoring

Most biomarker results compare your patient with strangers. Ours compare your patient with themselves.

A single blood test cannot tell you what a treatment did. Most proteins vary far more between people than within one person, so one result held against a population range hides whether your patient moved, in which direction, and by how much.

Proteox reads a series of draws against your patient's own baseline instead: ~3,000 plasma proteins each time, not a handful of routine markers. The dose can then follow the measured biological response, not only the symptoms.

Scope

What Proteox is, and what it is not.

Proteox is

  • Longitudinal, within-person monitoring of the plasma proteome
  • A way to see the biological response to a therapy a clinician has already prescribed
  • Clinician-facing reports that sit alongside existing clinical judgement
  • A service with a validation-first roadmap, on established, accredited measurement platforms

Proteox is not

  • A diagnostic or screening test
  • An age score or organ clock of any kind
  • A consumer product sold directly to individuals
  • A replacement for standard clinical monitoring
Science

The question is no longer whether a value is normal. It is whether it changed more than your patient's own noise.

Plasma proteomics has reached population scale. For the first time, thousands of proteins can be followed in one person, draw after draw. That changes the question we can ask.

Every protein drifts in the same person even when nothing has happened. Proteox measures that drift, so a reported change is one your patient does not produce by chance. Two things make this possible.

Within-person variation

Estimated protein by protein, so the threshold for a real change is specific to each protein, not one cut-off for the whole panel.

Population context

UK Biobank Pharma Proteomics Project data (~54,000 individuals, ~3,000 proteins) shows where your patient sits, and how unusual a change is.
